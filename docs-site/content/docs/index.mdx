**Slipstream** streams your desktop or games from a **Linux host** to your other devices - an
iPhone, an Android phone or TV, a Steam Deck - at low latency and at **each device's own resolution
and refresh rate**. Install the host, connect a client, and you're streaming.

People use it two ways:

- **Play** - stream games from a powerful Linux box to the couch, a Steam Deck, or a phone, with a
  self-filling game library and Moonlight compatibility when you want it.
- **Work** - use your real home or workstation desktop from another network over a LAN or VPN, with
  absolute mouse, clipboard, and Workstation / Hot-desk display presets.

What that means in practice:

- **Your device's exact mode.** The host spins up a virtual display sized to the client that's
  connecting, 1080p60 to one device, 1440p120 to another, at the same time. No letterboxing, no
  scaling, no juggling your real monitors.
- **Low latency, GPU end to end.** Frames go straight from the compositor to the GPU encoder,
  NVENC on NVIDIA, Vulkan Video or VAAPI on AMD and Intel, with no CPU copies on the paths where
  that's been validated (the
  [support matrix](/docs/support-matrix#zero-copy-capture-to-encode) says which), and over a
  transport tuned for responsiveness rather than throughput.
- **Works with the apps you already have.** Slipstream also speaks the GameStream protocol, so with
  `--gamestream` any **Moonlight** client connects, alongside a faster **native protocol** with
  dedicated apps for **iPhone, Android, and Steam Deck**.
- **Secure by default.** Hosts require a one-time PIN pairing; after that, devices reconnect on a
  pinned identity. No accounts, no cloud.

## What you need

- A **Linux host** with an NVIDIA, AMD, or Intel GPU (there's a software encoder to fall back on
  without one), running one of the [supported setups](/docs/requirements): **Ubuntu**, **Fedora**,
  **Arch**, **Bazzite**, or **SteamOS**. What each combination can actually do, codecs, HDR, 4:4:4,
  cursor, input, is in the [Support matrix](/docs/support-matrix).
- A **client device** to stream to: native apps for **iPhone**, **Android** (phone and Android TV),
  and **Steam Deck**, plus any device that runs **Moonlight**.
- Both on the **same network** (LAN or VPN). Slipstream is designed for a trusted local network -
  including the path from an office device back to a home desktop over a VPN.
